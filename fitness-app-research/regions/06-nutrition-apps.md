# nutrition-apps

## Key findings
- Logging friction is the single strongest predictor of abandonment: 50-70% of tracking-app users quit within 30 days, and users spending >15 min/day logging were 2.1x more likely to abandon by month three than those under 10 min. The steepest churn is in the first two weeks, so daily logging must take seconds, not minutes.
- MacroFactor is the industry benchmark for low-friction logging: it built a 'Food Logging Speed Index' (counting discrete taps per task across 20 apps) and won by using plate-based workflows (log multiple foods without relaunching the logger), instant serving-size selection on the food detail view, and unified search/barcode/AI-describe entry points. Copying its recents-first, minimal-tap flow is the highest-value UX pattern to steal.
- MacroFactor's adaptive TDEE algorithm is the standout calorie-budget pattern: it reverse-calculates true expenditure from logged intake vs. smoothed weight trend (e.g., 2,200 kcal logged + flat weight trend = ~2,200 TDEE), updates daily weighted toward recent data, and adjusts targets at a weekly check-in. It deliberately does NOT eat back Apple Watch active-energy calories, which sidesteps the double-counting mess entirely; it needs 2-3 weeks of data to calibrate.
- MyFitnessPal's approach of adding HealthKit exercise calories to the daily budget is a known failure mode: users complain about double-counting Apple Watch active energy vs. NEAT, confusing 'negative adjustments,' and inconsistent totals. MFP also triggered mass user exodus by paywalling the barcode scanner ($19.99/mo premium) and interrupting logging with ads.
- Database quality beats database size: MyFitnessPal's ~19M crowdsourced entries yield the worst tested calorie accuracy (~±6.8% variance) because unverified user entries are riddled with errors; Cronometer (curated NCCDB + USDA, staff-verified submissions, ~±3.5%) and MacroFactor (fully verified DB, ~±4.1%) are the accuracy leaders. For a personal app, a smaller verified database (USDA) plus barcode lookups (Open Food Facts) plus personal custom foods covers nearly all real usage.
- Free API stack is viable: USDA FoodData Central is free with an api.data.gov key at 1,000 requests/hour (search + detail endpoints; Foundation, SR Legacy, Branded, FNDDS data types); Open Food Facts is free/unlimited-ish (15 req/min/IP for barcode product lookups at /api/v2/product/{barcode}, 10 req/min for search, limits apply per end-user when calls come from a mobile app) with 2.5M+ barcoded products including nutriments per 100g and per serving. Nutritionix free tier is only 500 req/day and enterprise starts ~$1,850/mo; FatSecret's barcode lookup requires Premier tier — both are skippable.
- Photo-AI meal logging is a novelty, not a reliable primary input: Lose It's Snap It identified the food category only ~70% of the time and portion estimates swung 400-700 kcal on the same bowl of pasta depending on angle/lighting; HealthifyMe's Snap is 'a step in the right direction but not seamless.' Every teardown says photo AI degrades badly on mixed dishes. Barcode + recents + saved meals remain the workhorses.
- Gamification splits into two camps: Lose It/Yazio use streaks, badges, and challenges (Yazio adds 'streak freeze' days to prevent one missed day from killing motivation — the single most important streak mechanic), but users report motivation collapse when streaks break and Yazio's streak nag pop-ups are a top complaint. MacroFactor's 'adherence-neutral' philosophy is the counter-pattern: no red numbers when over target, no punishment for missed days, weekly coaching continues regardless of adherence — reviewers credit this with retention because it removes shame spirals.
- Cronometer proves depth has a UX cost: 84 tracked micronutrients from lab-analyzed NCCDB data (77 nutrients/food) makes it the accuracy gold standard, but its interface is widely called complex/clunky/dated with a slow search-and-confirm workflow — a warning against surfacing too much data in the daily logging path.
- The lowest-friction daily-logging pattern consensus across teardowns: (1) recents/favorites surfaced immediately on open (most meals repeat), (2) one-tap re-log of previous meals or 'copy yesterday', (3) barcode scan free and one tap from home, (4) saved multi-food meals/recipes, (5) quick-add calories+macros for estimates, (6) default serving pre-selected from last use. Search should be the fallback, not the primary path.
- HealthifyMe's differentiators are an Indian-food-focused database, HealthifySnap photo logging, and the Ria AI chat coach; complaints center on generic AI advice, aggressive upselling, and human-coach turnover. Its main transferable idea is conversational/natural-language food entry ('2 rotis and dal') as a logging modality.
- Yazio's strengths: multi-path logging (search, barcode, recents, favorites, AI photo, nutrition-label photo scan), clean visual design, and forgiving streaks; weaknesses: no offline mode, mobile-only, thin free tier, and intrusive streak/upsell notifications — evidence that gamification prompts must stay quiet.
- Lose It's free tier is the most complete mainstream option (free barcode scanning, 27M-food DB, basic macros) and its weekly-flexible calorie budget (bank calories for social days) is a well-liked budgeting pattern; premium gates photo/voice logging, micronutrients, and intermittent fasting.

## Specifics
- USDA FoodData Central API: free key from fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-key-signup (proxied via api.data.gov), 1,000 requests/hour/key, DEMO_KEY limited to 30/hr and 50/day; check X-RateLimit-Limit and X-RateLimit-Remaining headers; endpoints: GET /v1/foods/search, GET /v1/food/{fdcId}; data types: Foundation, SR Legacy, Branded, Survey (FNDDS); ~400k+ foods; public-domain data (no attribution burden)
- Open Food Facts API: GET https://world.openfoodfacts.org/api/v2/product/{barcode} with ?fields=product_name,brands,nutriments,serving_size,nutrition_grades to trim payload; nutriments object gives per-100g and per-serving values; rate limits 15 req/min/IP product reads, 10 req/min search — applied per end user for mobile apps, so a personal app is effectively unlimited; 2.5M+ products; ODbL license (fine for personal use)
- Nutritionix API: 500 requests/day free tier, natural-language endpoint (natural/nutrients parses '2 eggs and toast'), 1.9M foods, enterprise from ~$1,850/month — not worth it for a personal app
- FatSecret Platform API: barcode method food.find_id_for_barcode.v2 returns full food object, but barcode is Premier-tier only (free Basic returns 'Missing scope' Error 14); Premier Free tier exists for students/startups with attribution — user has a .edu email so could qualify
- MacroFactor Food Logging Speed Index (FLSI): objective framework counting discrete actions (taps/selections) per common logging task, benchmarked across 20 apps; key tap-savers: plate-based multi-food logging without relaunching the logger, speed-mode (food detail view opens straight to serving-size picker) vs context-mode (Plate as focal point), remembered last-used serving size
- MacroFactor adaptive expenditure: TDEE derived from logged intake vs. exponentially-smoothed weight trend, updated daily, weekly check-in adjusts calorie/macro targets toward target rate of weight change; ignores wearable active-energy for budgeting (uses initial estimate only until enough data); needs ~2-3 weeks to calibrate; HealthKit used for weight import
- MacroFactor 'adherence-neutral' design: no red numbers or warning states when over target, no streaks, missed days don't degrade coaching — algorithm still adjusts correctly because it uses actual logged data (source: macrofactor.com/adherence-neutral/ and strongerbyscience.com/macrofactor-algorithms-philosophy/)
- MyFitnessPal: ~19M-food database of which ~14M are 'verified' green-checkmark entries; user-submitted entries unverified and error-prone (worst tested accuracy ~±6.8%); barcode scanner paywalled Oct 2022 ($19.99/mo, $79.99/yr); 2026 'Meal Scan' adds photo-upload AI logging (iOS); HealthKit 'Calorie Adjustment' compares partner total daily burn vs MFP's projection, optional 'negative adjustments' setting to prevent overcounting — chronic user confusion
- MyFitnessPal exercise-calorie model: budget = goal + logged exercise + device adjustment; community threads document double-counting between Apple Watch workouts, steps, and manually logged exercise — the pattern to avoid
- Cronometer: 84 nutrients tracked; NCCDB lab-analyzed database (77 nutrients/food) + USDA; user food submissions require nutrition-label photo and staff review; complaints: clunky recipe logging, dated UI, slow search-and-confirm, full-screen video ads on free tier
- Lose It!: 27M-food DB, free barcode scanning on all platforms, free tier includes unlimited logging + basic macros; Snap It photo logging ~70% food-category accuracy, portion estimates varied 400-700 kcal on identical dish; streaks + badges + weekly challenges; weekly-flexible calorie budget (bank calories across days); Premium $39.99/yr gates photo/voice logging, micronutrients, IF timer, health-metric tracking
- Yazio: logging paths = search, barcode, recents, favorites, AI photo tracker, nutrition-label photo capture; 'streak freeze' earned after several days of logging so one missed day doesn't reset; complaints: streak/upsell pop-up spam, no offline mode, thin free tier; strong European food coverage
- HealthifyMe: HealthifySnap photo-AI logging tuned for Indian/international dishes; Ria AI chat coach for real-time feedback; complaints: generic AI advice, coach turnover, heavy upselling; strongest Indian-food database of the six
- Abandonment research: 50-70% of tracking-app users quit within 30 days; median 70% churn by day 100 across health apps with steepest drop in first two weeks; >15 min/day logging = 2.1x abandonment risk vs <10 min; every extra input field adds measurable friction (sources: Sahha churn analysis, Springer 'Is Burden Always Bad?', SnappyMeal arXiv 2511.03907)
- SnappyMeal (arXiv 2511.03907, longitudinal multimodal AI logging study): supports multimodal input (photo + voice + text) reducing burden but confirms users still correct AI outputs; low-burden approaches trade accuracy for adherence and adherence usually wins
- Consensus lowest-friction logging stack: open app -> today screen with recents/frequents one tap away; 'copy previous meal/day'; saved meals (multi-food bundles); quick-add kcal+P/C/F; barcode scanner one tap from home screen; last-used serving size pre-selected; search as fallback with verified-first ranking
- Calorie-budget patterns observed: static formula budget (Yazio), static + eat-back exercise calories from HealthKit (MFP, Lose It — double-counting-prone), weekly banking budget (Lose It), adaptive TDEE from weight trend + intake (MacroFactor — most robust with an Apple Watch because it never touches active energy)
- Streak mechanics worth copying: Yazio streak-freeze token; Lose It weekly challenges/badges; anti-pattern: hard streak reset (documented motivation collapse) and notification nagging

## Recommendations
- Build logging around recents/favorites/saved-meals surfaced on app open, one-tap re-log, and 'copy yesterday' — treat text search as fallback. Target <15 seconds per meal and count taps for every flow (MacroFactor's FLSI mindset); pre-select the last-used serving size for every food.
- Use USDA FoodData Central (free key, 1,000 req/hr) for search/generic foods and Open Food Facts /api/v2/product/{barcode} for barcode scans (VisionKit/AVFoundation scanner on iOS is free — no SDK needed); cache every hit into a local Core Data/SQLite personal food store so the app works offline and repeat logs never hit the network. Skip Nutritionix and FatSecret.
- Copy MacroFactor's calorie budget model, not MyFitnessPal's: compute adaptive TDEE from logged intake vs. exponentially-smoothed HealthKit weight trend (weekly target adjustment), and do NOT add Apple Watch active energy back into the daily budget — this avoids the double-counting complaints that dominate MFP forums. Use HealthKit activeEnergyBurned only for display/initial TDEE seed before 2-3 weeks of data exist.
- Adopt adherence-neutral presentation: never render over-budget numbers in red or warning states; in your Claude-style palette, keep the coral #D97757 as a neutral accent and avoid a 'failure' color entirely. Over-target is just information.
- For the RPG layer, award XP for the act of logging (any log = XP), not for staying under calories; implement Yazio-style streak-freeze tokens (earned every N days) so a missed day never hard-resets the streak/level, and keep gamification notifications off by default.
- Offer quick-add (calories + optional macros) and multi-food saved meals from day one; skip photo-AI logging — accuracy is ~70% on category and wildly off on portions, and for a personal app the maintenance cost isn't worth it. If desired later, natural-language entry via an LLM API is the cheaper novelty.
- Track only calories + protein/carbs/fat in the primary UI (Cronometer shows deep micronutrient data buries the daily flow); if you want micronutrients, put them on a secondary detail screen fed by USDA Foundation/SR Legacy data which already includes them.
- Support barcode scanning free and one tap from the home screen — its paywalling is the single most hated move in this category (MFP exodus), and it's the second-most-used logging path after recents.
