# distribution (installing a personal iOS+watchOS app without the App Store, 2026)

## Key findings
- Xcode free provisioning (signing with a free Apple ID 'Personal Team') is the baseline path: provisioning profiles expire 7 days from issuance, a maximum of 3 apps signed with free development profiles can be installed on the device at once, at most 10 App IDs can be registered per rolling 7-day window, and roughly 3 test devices per platform are allowed. After expiry the app must be rebuilt/reinstalled from Xcode.
- Critically for this project, HealthKit IS available to free personal teams — Apple's own 'Supported capabilities (iOS)' table lists HealthKit, HomeKit, background modes, Wallet, and Siri as available to the free 'Apple Developer' membership tier; multiple sources confirm HealthKit works for personal on-device testing with free provisioning (you just can't distribute).
- Capabilities that do NOT work on a free personal team (Xcode errors 'Personal development teams do not support the X capability'): Push Notifications (APNs) and iCloud/CloudKit are the most commonly confirmed blockers; Apple Pay, Sign in with Apple, and In-App Purchase are also paid-only in practice. Local notifications (UNUserNotificationCenter) need no entitlement and work fine on free accounts — so reminders/streak nudges are still possible without APNs.
- The HealthKit background-delivery entitlement (com.apple.developer.healthkit.background-delivery, required for HKObserverQuery background updates since iOS 15) is part of the HealthKit capability toggle in Xcode; no source documents it being blocked for personal teams, but it is also not explicitly confirmed — treat passive background sync as 'probably works, verify on device; fall back to refresh-on-foreground.'
- A watchOS companion app CAN be deployed with a free account, but Xcode is effectively the ONLY way to sideload a watch app (AltStore/SideStore/Sideloadly sign iPhone IPAs and do not install watchOS companions). Forum evidence shows the iOS+watch pair roughly counts as one-to-two slots against the 3-app free limit, and the watch build also expires every 7 days.
- When the 7-day profile expires the app stays on the phone but refuses to launch ('Unable to Verify App'); NO data is deleted. Rebuilding from Xcode with the same bundle ID over-installs and iOS preserves the app container (Documents, UserDefaults, Core Data/SwiftData stores). HealthKit data is stored in the system Health database, not the app sandbox, so workouts/routes/samples written by the app survive expiry, reinstall, and even full app deletion; HealthKit authorization grants also persist for the same bundle ID.
- The paid Apple Developer Program ($99/yr) removes all friction: development provisioning profiles last ~1 year, 100 registered devices per device type per year, all entitlements (push, iCloud sync), Ad Hoc distribution (install a signed IPA on up to 100 registered devices, 1-year profile), and TestFlight.
- TestFlight requires the paid account: builds go through App Store Connect, internal testers (up to 100) get 90-day build validity with automatic updates and no cable — the most comfortable 'personal app' loop short of the App Store, at the cost of periodic re-uploads and light beta review for external groups (internal testing needs no review).
- AltStore/SideStore/Sideloadly automate the 7-day refresh but are bound by the same free-Apple-ID limits (7-day certs, 3 apps): AltStore refreshes via AltServer running on a Mac/PC on the same Wi-Fi (fails if the computer is asleep); SideStore refreshes on-device after one-time setup using a pairing file plus a local loopback VPN helper (StikDebug/StosVPN) — convenient but fragile across iOS updates; AltStore Classic 2.3 (2026) added full on-device sideloading without AltServer; Sideloadly auto-refreshes when the device reconnects to the computer. All of these install pre-built IPAs, which is awkward for an app you're actively developing in Xcode anyway.
- EU vs US (2026): DMA alternative app marketplaces (AltStore PAL, Epic Games Store, Onside) and direct Web Distribution exist only for users with an EU-registered Apple ID physically located in the EU; nothing equivalent exists in the US. Even in the EU these routes require a paid developer account, Apple notarization, and (for web distribution) multi-year membership/large install-base eligibility — irrelevant for a personal one-user app. For a US-based user, the realistic options remain: free Xcode provisioning, $99 + TestFlight/Ad Hoc, or AltStore-family refreshers.
- iOS 16+ requires Developer Mode enabled on the iPhone (Settings > Privacy & Security) and trusting the developer certificate; the Apple Watch must be paired and enabled for development, and building for a Watch Ultra 3 (watchOS 26) requires current Xcode 26 on the Mac. After first cable pairing, Xcode can deploy over Wi-Fi, making the weekly re-sign a ~2-minute wireless ritual.
- Known free-account gotchas: 'maximum number of apps for free development profiles has been reached' is triggered even by iCloud-offloaded dev apps still counting toward the 3-app limit (delete or reinstall them to free slots); reusing the SAME bundle ID for all personal projects avoids burning the 10-per-week App ID quota; MapKit works on free accounts (only turn-by-turn routing-provider capability is restricted), so Strava-like GPS route maps are fully available.

## Specifics
- Free personal team limits: 7-day provisioning profile expiry; 3 concurrently installed dev apps; 10 App IDs per rolling 7 days; ~3 devices per platform
- Xcode error strings to expect: 'The maximum number of apps for free development profiles has been reached' (code -402620383 / 0xE8008021); 'Unable to add App ID because the 10 App ID limit in 7 days has been exceeded'; 'Personal development teams do not support the Push Notifications capability'
- Entitlements confirmed usable free: com.apple.developer.healthkit (HealthKit capability), background modes (UIBackgroundModes incl. location for GPS route tracking), HomeKit, Siri, Wallet, keychain-access-groups
- Entitlements requiring paid $99 account: aps-environment (push/APNs), com.apple.developer.icloud-container-identifiers (CloudKit), Apple Pay, Sign in with Apple, In-App Purchase
- Uncertain on free tier: com.apple.developer.healthkit.background-delivery (needed for HKHealthStore.enableBackgroundDelivery(for:frequency:) + HKObserverQuery in background) — verify on device; also historical mixed reports for App Groups on personal teams
- MapKit fully works on free tier; only the routing-app (directions-provider) capability is paid — GPS route rendering a la Strava is unaffected; Core Location background updates work with the standard background-modes entitlement
- Local notifications (UNUserNotificationCenter) require no entitlement — use instead of APNs push on a free account
- Profile expiry behavior: app icon remains, launch blocked with 'Unable to Verify App'; app sandbox (Documents/UserDefaults/SwiftData) preserved on re-install with same bundle ID; iOS treats same-bundle-ID reinstall as the same app and keeps container files
- HealthKit data model: all samples (HKWorkout, HKWorkoutRoute, HKQuantitySample) live in the central Health store owned by the OS — they survive app deletion/expiry entirely; read/write authorization persists per bundle ID
- watchOS: watch companion deployable free via Xcode only; AltStore/SideStore/Sideloadly cannot install watchOS apps; iOS+watch pair consumes app-limit slots; watch build expires on the same 7-day cycle; Watch Ultra 3 (watchOS 26) needs Xcode 26
- Paid ADP ($99/yr): 1-year development profiles, 100 devices per device type per membership year, Ad Hoc IPA distribution to 100 registered UDIDs, all entitlements, App Store Connect + TestFlight
- TestFlight: builds valid 90 days from upload, internal testers (App Store Connect users, up to 100) need no beta review, automatic OTA updates; after 90 days 'Expired Build' until a new upload
- AltStore: needs AltServer on Mac/PC + same Wi-Fi for background refresh; fails silently if computer asleep; AltStore Classic 2.3 beta (2026) adds on-device sideloading without AltServer
- SideStore: fork of AltStore; on-device refresh via device pairing file + loopback VPN helper (StikDebug / StosVPN / LocalDevVPN); same 7-day/3-app free-ID limits; breaks occasionally on iOS updates
- Sideloadly: PC/Mac tool, free Apple ID signing with anisette data, auto-refresh when device connected to the running computer; keep same Apple ID + custom bundle ID across updates to preserve data
- EU DMA (2026): alternative marketplaces AltStore PAL, Epic Games Store, Onside (EU + Japan since 2026-02-17); Web Distribution needs Alternative Terms Addendum, paid membership, Apple notarization; Core Technology Fee EUR 0.50 per first annual install for marketplace operators; requires EU Apple ID + physical EU presence — none of it available in the US
- iPhone setup requirements: Developer Mode toggle (iOS 16+, Settings > Privacy & Security > Developer Mode, requires reboot), trust cert under Settings > General > VPN & Device Management; Wi-Fi deploy from Xcode after first wired pairing
- Debug trick: Console.app filter 'MIFreeProfileValidatedAppTracker' shows which bundle IDs count against the free 3-app limit; iCloud-offloaded dev apps still count until deleted

## Recommendations
- Ship v1 with Xcode free provisioning: HealthKit, workout writing, GPS/MapKit routes, background location, and local notifications all work on a Personal Team. Keep ONE fixed bundle ID forever (e.g. com.anuj.fitrpg) so reinstalls preserve UserDefaults/SwiftData and HealthKit authorizations and you never burn the 10-App-ID weekly quota.
- Architect all durable state into HealthKit (workouts, routes via HKWorkoutRoute, body metrics) plus a local SwiftData store; never depend on iCloud (blocked on free tier) — this makes the 7-day expiry a non-event since HealthKit data lives outside the app sandbox.
- Design the RPG layer to recompute level/XP from HealthKit history on launch (idempotent derivation rather than only incrementally stored state) — then even a worst-case container loss costs nothing.
- Accept a weekly ~2-minute ritual: after first cable pairing, enable Xcode Wi-Fi deploy and just hit Run once a week; optionally script it (xcodebuild + devicectl) on the Mac. AltStore/SideStore are poor fits here because the app is under active development in Xcode and they can't install the watchOS companion anyway.
- Build the watchOS companion via Xcode (only channel that supports watch apps); keep total installed dev apps at or under 3 including the watch pair, and confirm Developer Mode is on for both iPhone and Watch Ultra 3 (needs Xcode 26 for watchOS 26).
- Do not rely on APNs push or CloudKit; use UNUserNotificationCenter local notifications for streaks/reminders and BGTaskScheduler + HealthKit observer queries for refresh. Prototype com.apple.developer.healthkit.background-delivery early on the free account — if it silently fails, fall back to refreshing metrics on app foreground and via watch-app writes.
- If the weekly re-sign or lack of reliable background delivery becomes annoying, the single best upgrade is the $99/yr Apple Developer Program: 1-year dev profiles (install once, forget), TestFlight internal testing (90-day builds, OTA updates, no Mac needed to update), plus push and iCloud sync unlocked. There is no EU-style sideloading alternative in the US in 2026.
- Ignore EU DMA marketplaces/web distribution entirely: US-based, single-user, and those channels require paid membership + notarization anyway.
