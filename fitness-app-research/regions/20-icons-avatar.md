# icons-avatar

## Key findings
- The single most Claude-consistent avatar approach is NOT licensed art at all: a flat geometric mascot drawn natively in SwiftUI (Shape/Canvas) using Anthropic's actual brand tokens (ivory #faf9f5 bg, dark #141413, coral #d97757 accent, secondary blue #6a9bcc / green #788c5d, light gray #e8e6dc). Claude's starburst mascot works because it is one flat accent color, simple silhouette, no outlines, no gradients — personality comes from shape and motion, not detail. A solo dev with no illustration skill can produce this; it is tintable, resolution-independent, and animatable with SwiftUI springs.
- Finch's art direction formula (the gold standard for 'cute without clutter'): ONE mascot character, soft rounded silhouettes, limited pastel palette, dot eyes and big-head/small-body proportions, and progression expressed as LIFE STAGES (egg -> chick -> toddler -> teen -> adult) rather than accumulating gear. Cuteness is achieved through proportion and micro-animation (blink, bounce), never through detail density. This maps directly onto a minimal fitness RPG: evolve the silhouette per level tier instead of rendering an inventory.
- Habitica is the cautionary counter-example on both art direction and licensing: its paper-doll layered pixel sprites (body + hair + armor + weapon layers composited) enable equipment progression but produce visual clutter, and its art assets are CC-BY-NC-SA 3.0 (HabitRPG-designed) / CC-BY-SA 3.0 (Mozilla BrowserQuest-derived) per its LICENSE file — share-alike, non-commercial terms make them the wrong assets to copy even for a personal app. Borrow the layered-sprite TECHNIQUE, not the assets, and cap visible layers.
- SF Symbols should cover 100% of functional UI iconography: 7,000+ symbols, 9 weights, 3 scales, four rendering modes (monochrome, hierarchical, palette, multicolor), plus Variable Color/Variable Draw for progress indication and built-in animations (bounce, wiggle, breathe, draw on/off, Magic Replace for symbol transitions). Restrictions: symbols may only be used inside Apple-platform apps and not as logos/app icons — irrelevant for a sideloaded personal app. Hierarchical mode tinted #d97757 on ivory is essentially the Claude aesthetic for free.
- SF Symbols cannot express RPG flavor (swords, potions, laurels, XP crystals); the best fill-in source is game-icons.net — thousands of RPG-themed SVG icons under CC-BY (attribution to individual artists like lorc, delapouite, sbed), with an official repo (github.com/game-icons/icons) including a colorize-svgs.sh recoloring script. Recolored to flat monochrome terracotta these read as classy, not gamey.
- Raw platform emoji is the wrong choice for a controlled minimal palette — Apple's glossy multi-color emoji visually clash with a flat cream/terracotta UI. If emoji-style glyphs are wanted (e.g., food categories), OpenMoji (CC BY-SA 4.0, SVG + PNG 618/72px, both color AND black outline variants) provides a flat, consistent, restylable alternative; its black-outline set can be stroke-tinted to brand colors.
- DiceBear is the strongest ready-made avatar system for a no-illustration-skills developer: 50 styles, deterministic seed-string -> SVG generation, usable fully offline via native libraries (JS/PHP/Python/Rust/Go/Dart) or a free no-account HTTP API (https://api.dicebear.com/10.x/[style]/svg?seed=...), self-hostable via Docker. Styles span anime-ish flat vector (Adventurer, Lorelei), illustration (Open Peeps), pixel art (Pixel Art), robots (Bottts), and text (Initials); each style carries its own creator license (see dicebear.com/licenses — many require only attribution). Practical pattern for iOS: generate the SVG once per level/variant, convert to PDF or PNG, and bundle in the asset catalog (no SVG runtime needed).
- Kenney remains the canonical CC0 (public-domain, no attribution) game-asset source for a solo dev — tens of thousands of assets across packs like Toon Characters, Tiny Dungeon, Pixel Platformer, Emotes Pack, Game Icons, Board Game Icons, and UI Pack; itch.io additionally hosts many CC0 pixel-character packs (e.g., Ninja Adventure, Sprout Lands). Pixel sprites at 16-32px render crisply in SwiftUI via Image(...).interpolation(.none) with integer scaling. (Kenney.nl and itch.io were unreachable through this session's proxy; details from established knowledge — verify pack lists on kenney.nl/assets.)
- Level-badge best practice for this aesthetic: flat circular medallion = thin ring stroke + single centered glyph + level numeral in a serif face (very Anthropic: serif numerals on ivory), with tier progression expressed through the brand palette sequence (gray -> coral -> blue -> green -> dark) and/or added geometry (second ring, laurel ticks) rather than metallic 3D materials. Apple Fitness's skeuomorphic 3D medals are the counter-example. Locked state = 15-25% opacity outline version (standard Duolingo/Apple pattern); progress-to-next-level via an SF Symbol with Variable Color or a single terracotta ring arc.
- Pixel art is the most forgiving style for non-illustrators who want to make small edits themselves: at 16x16/32x32 with a locked 4-6 color palette (sampled from the brand tokens), even hand-edits in a free editor (Aseprite $ or Piskel/Pixelorama free) stay coherent. Full anime-style illustration is the LEAST solo-dev-viable direction; if an anime flavor is required, use DiceBear Adventurer/Lorelei flat-vector styles or commission nothing and accept chibi/flat proxies.
- Licensing posture for a never-distributed sideloaded personal app: practically nothing is enforced, but the clean stack is CC0 (Kenney) + ISC (Lucide, 1600+ stroke icons, fork of Feather, free for any use — no official Swift package, ship as SVG->asset-catalog PDFs) + CC-BY with an in-app about-screen credit (game-icons.net, DiceBear attribution styles) + CC BY-SA (OpenMoji). Avoid CC-BY-NC-SA Habitica art and never use SF Symbols outside the app UI.

## Specifics
- Anthropic brand tokens (official skill): dark #141413, light/ivory #faf9f5, mid gray #b0aea5, light gray #e8e6dc, accent orange/coral #d97757, secondary blue #6a9bcc, tertiary green #788c5d; headings Poppins-like geometric sans, body Lora-like serif
- SF Symbols: 7,000+ symbols, 9 weights, 3 scales; rendering modes .monochrome / .hierarchical / .palette / .multicolor; Variable Color + Variable Draw for progress; animations: bounce, wiggle, rotate, breathe, draw-on/draw-off, Magic Replace
- SF Symbols fitness/RPG-adjacent identifiers: figure.run, figure.walk, figure.strength.training.traditional, figure.pool.swim, figure.outdoor.cycle, flame.fill, heart.fill, bolt.fill, fork.knife, moon.zzz.fill, bed.double.fill, map.fill, location.fill, trophy.fill, medal.fill, crown.fill, star.circle.fill, shield.fill, sparkles, chart.bar.fill
- SwiftUI symbol APIs: Image(systemName:), .symbolRenderingMode(.hierarchical), .symbolVariant(.fill), .symbolEffect(.bounce), .symbolEffect(.variableColor), foregroundStyle with brand Color
- SF Symbols license: use only within Apple-platform app UI; cannot be exported as standalone logos/app icons or modified beyond scaling
- DiceBear: 50 styles, seed-deterministic SVG; offline native libs for JS/PHP/Python/Rust/Go/Dart with byte-identical output; free HTTP API https://api.dicebear.com/10.x/[style]/svg?seed=[input]; Docker self-host; per-style licenses at dicebear.com/licenses (many attribution-only)
- DiceBear styles confirmed in repo: Adventurer (anime-ish flat vector), Lorelei (flat portrait), Open Peeps (hand-drawn illustration), Pixel Art (8-bit), Bottts (robots), Initials (typographic)
- game-icons.net / github.com/game-icons/icons: RPG-themed SVG icon library, CC-BY license with per-artist attribution (folders by artist: lorc, delapouite, sbed, ...), includes colorize-svgs.sh recolor script and PNG rasterization scripts; themes tagged boardgame / dungeons-and-dragons / gamedev
- Lucide: 1600+ stroke-based SVG icons, ISC license (free commercial + personal), fork of Feather; official packages for React/Vue/Svelte/React-Native etc. but NO native Swift package — ship SVGs as asset-catalog PDFs or convert to SwiftUI Paths
- OpenMoji: CC BY-SA 4.0 graphics (code LGPL-3.0), color + black-outline variants, SVG plus PNG at 618x618 and 72x72, npm distribution; outline variant can be tinted to brand colors
- Habitica LICENSE (verbatim findings): code GPL v3; 'Assets and content designed for HabitRPG are licensed under CC-BY-NC-SA 3.0'; Mozilla BrowserQuest-derived assets CC-BY-SA 3.0; avatar art lives in separate habitica-images submodule; avatar system = layered paper-doll sprite compositing (body/hair/armor/weapon)
- Finch progression pattern: mascot life-stage evolution (egg -> baby -> child -> teen -> adult) driven by XP ('energy'), plus optional cosmetic accessories; one character per screen, pastel palette, micro-animations for reward moments
- Kenney (CC0, no attribution required) relevant packs: Toon Characters, Tiny Dungeon, Pixel Platformer, Emotes Pack, Game Icons, Board Game Icons, UI Pack; itch.io CC0 pixel packs: Ninja Adventure (pixel-boy), Sprout Lands — verify on kenney.nl/assets and itch.io (blocked via this session's proxy)
- Pixel-art rendering in SwiftUI: Image("sprite").interpolation(.none).resizable().scaledToFit() with integer multiples of native sprite size (16/32px) to avoid blur
- Procedural in-app avatar technique: SwiftUI Canvas/Shape mascot composed of 3-6 primitives (rounded body blob, dot eyes, starburst aura); level tier swaps/adds primitives; Color tokens from brand palette; spring animation (.spring(response:0.35, dampingFraction:0.6)) for level-up pop
- Badge recipe: 64pt Circle stroked 2pt #d97757 on #faf9f5, centered game-icons glyph or SF Symbol at 28pt monochrome, serif level numeral (or Roman numeral) below; tier colors cycle gray #b0aea5 -> coral #d97757 -> blue #6a9bcc -> green #788c5d -> dark #141413; locked = outline at 20% opacity
- XP/progress visual: single terracotta ring arc around avatar (one ring, not three, to differentiate from Apple's Activity rings), or SF Symbol Variable Color fill for streaks
- Free pixel editors for tiny self-made edits: Piskel (web, free), Pixelorama (open source), LibreSprite (free Aseprite fork); Aseprite (~$20) as paid option
- SVG-to-SwiftUI conversion routes: (a) drop SVG->PDF into Xcode asset catalog with 'Preserve Vector Data', (b) convert to SwiftUI Path via svg-to-swiftui web converters, (c) rasterize at 2x/3x PNG
- Art-direction rule set distilled from Claude/Finch: 1 mascot, <=2 accent colors per screen, flat fills, no outlines/gradients/drop-shadows, silhouette-first design, generous whitespace, motion instead of detail for delight

## Recommendations
- Primary art direction: build the avatar as a flat geometric Claude-style mascot rendered natively in SwiftUI (Canvas/Shapes) with the exact brand tokens (#faf9f5 bg, #d97757 accent, #141413 ink) — zero external assets, no illustration skill needed, fully animatable; express leveling as 5 life-stage silhouette evolutions (Finch pattern) plus small earned accessories, never a gear inventory (Habitica anti-pattern).
- If the user insists on pixel/anime flavor, plan B: bundle DiceBear-generated SVGs (Pixel Art or Adventurer style, generated once per level and stored as asset-catalog PDFs/PNGs) or a CC0 Kenney/itch.io sprite sheet rendered with .interpolation(.none); constrain sprites to a 4-6 color palette sampled from the brand tokens so they sit on the cream UI.
- Use SF Symbols for every functional icon (workouts figure.run etc., nutrition fork.knife, sleep moon.zzz.fill, routes map.fill), hierarchical rendering mode tinted #d97757; exploit Variable Color for streak/progress glyphs and .symbolEffect(.bounce) for reward moments — this alone delivers most of the 'minimal but alive' feel.
- Source RPG-flavored badge glyphs (sword, shield, laurel, potion, crystal) from game-icons.net (CC-BY, add an attribution line in Settings/About), recolored to flat monochrome; do NOT use emoji in the core UI, and use OpenMoji black-outline variants if emoji-like food glyphs are needed.
- Adopt the flat medallion badge system: thin-ring circle + single glyph + serif numeral, tiers advancing through the brand palette (gray->coral->blue->green->dark), locked badges as 20%-opacity outlines; skip Apple-Fitness-style 3D metallic medals as off-aesthetic.
- Show XP as one terracotta ring arc around the avatar (deliberately one ring, distinct from Apple's three Activity rings) with a level chip in serif numerals.
- Keep the license stack clean even though the app is personal/sideloaded: CC0 (Kenney) + ISC (Lucide) + CC-BY-with-credit (game-icons, some DiceBear styles); never copy Habitica art (CC-BY-NC-SA) and never repurpose SF Symbols outside app UI.
- Total asset budget achievable with no illustrator: ~1 procedural mascot (code), ~10-15 badge glyphs (game-icons SVGs), 0 custom icons (all SF Symbols), optional 1 DiceBear fallback set — a weekend of asset work, all free.
