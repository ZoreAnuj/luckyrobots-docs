# energy-models (calorie/energy expenditure)

## Key findings
- Apple Watch heart rate is accurate (~2-6.5% error, MAPE ~4.4%, within the <10% clinical standard) but ENERGY EXPENDITURE is not. Across validation studies the median/mean absolute error for calories is ~27-31%, and a 2024 meta-analysis of ~56 studies (models through Series 8) found the Apple Watch overestimates energy by ~28% on average. This is the single most important fact for the app: HR data can be trusted; calorie data should be treated as a rough estimate, never as ground truth.
- The Stanford Shcherbina et al. 2017 study (J. Personalized Medicine, 7(2):3) tested 7 wrist wearables against indirect calorimetry: NO device achieved an acceptable energy-expenditure error. Apple Watch had the LOWEST error of the group but still ~27% median error; the worst device was ~93%. Error was lowest for walking/running and highest for cycling (~52% in the Stanford data). Conclusion of the field: consumer wrist devices are validated for HR, not for calories.
- Error grows with activity type and intensity: overestimation during running can exceed 50%; cycling errors are largest because wrist accelerometry poorly captures leg-dominant work. Accuracy improved only modestly across six hardware generations (Series 1 ~31.5% error to Series 6 ~26.2%), so newer watches (incl. Ultra) do not solve the problem.
- Apple's own calorimetry works by fusing PPG heart rate, accelerometer (wrist motion / step cadence), GPS (for outdoor workouts), workout type, and user demographics (age, sex, height, weight) into proprietary regression models calibrated against indirect calorimetry. During workouts it uses HR-driven models; outside workouts it leans on accelerometer + step-based models. Personalization is limited to the demographic inputs plus learned resting HR/fitness signals.
- Apple splits energy into RESTING energy (basalEnergyBurned, an estimated BMR from age/sex/height/weight, active essentially 24h) and ACTIVE energy (activeEnergyBurned, the incremental burn above rest from movement). The red Move ring counts ACTIVE only. Total daily = resting + active. This split is critical because naive apps double count by adding a full TDEE to the Watch's active number.
- MacroFactor-style adaptive TDEE is the most honest expenditure model available to a personal app: it ignores the Watch's calorie estimate entirely and instead back-calculates TDEE from the energy-balance equation (weight change = intake - expenditure) using a rolling window of logged intake and a smoothed weight trend (~2-4 weeks). It self-corrects for metabolic adaptation and activity changes and needs no wearable calorie data at all. It requires ~2 weeks of honest intake + daily-ish weigh-ins to stabilize.
- The honesty argument for trend-based energy balance: no single day's intake, BMR, or active burn is knowable to better than ~10-20%, but because those errors are roughly stable in direction and magnitude, they largely cancel over a 14-28 day window, making the multi-week trend reliable to a few percent even when every daily number is wrong. The correct display philosophy is 'the trend is the truth; the daily number is an estimate.'
- Prediction equations for BMR/RMR: Mifflin-St Jeor is the most accurate for the general population (within 10% of measured RMR in ~71% of people, near-zero group bias). Katch-McArdle and Cunningham use lean body mass instead of total weight and are better for lean/athletic users IF body-fat % is known accurately, but Cunningham showed the largest mean deviation (~-16.6%) in mixed populations. All equations fail ~1/3 of individuals and degrade at BMI extremes.
- Double counting is the #1 implementation pitfall. Apple Watch independently writes real-time activeEnergyBurned to HealthKit. If a third-party app ALSO writes its own estimated active energy (or writes a full TDEE into activeEnergyBurned), HealthKit sums or mis-prioritizes them and inflates totals (documented case: an app wrote 2200 kcal TDEE to ActiveEnergyBurned and Health added 500 kcal of Watch burn on top = 2700). Manual entries > Watch > iPhone motion > third-party in Apple's priority order.
- MET-based estimates (calories = METs x 3.5 x kg / 200 per minute, from the 2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities) are a transparent, explainable fallback for workouts the Watch didn't track, but standard METs are not corrected for individual age/sex/body-mass; a 'corrected MET' (Kozey et al.) scales the MET by the ratio of the person's predicted RMR to the 3.5 ml/kg/min = 1 MET assumption for better personalization.

## Specifics
- HealthKit identifiers: HKQuantityTypeIdentifier.activeEnergyBurned (kcal, the Move ring / incremental burn), HKQuantityTypeIdentifier.basalEnergyBurned (kcal, estimated resting/BMR), HKQuantityTypeIdentifier.dietaryEnergyConsumed (kcal intake), HKQuantityTypeIdentifier.bodyMass, HKQuantityTypeIdentifier.leanBodyMass, HKQuantityTypeIdentifier.bodyFatPercentage, HKQuantityTypeIdentifier.vo2Max, HKQuantityTypeIdentifier.restingHeartRate.
- HKWorkout exposes totalEnergyBurned (historically active energy for the session) and via HKWorkoutBuilder/statistics(for:) you read per-workout activeEnergyBurned and basalEnergyBurned separately. Prefer reading Apple's samples over writing your own.
- Total daily energy shown in Apple Health = basalEnergyBurned (resting, ~24h) + activeEnergyBurned (active). Move ring = activeEnergyBurned only. Do NOT add these to a separately-computed TDEE.
- Mifflin-St Jeor BMR (kcal/day): men = 10*wt_kg + 6.25*height_cm - 5*age + 5; women = 10*wt_kg + 6.25*height_cm - 5*age - 161. (weight coefficient sometimes cited as 9.99.)
- Katch-McArdle BMR = 370 + 21.6 * LBM_kg, where LBM = weight * (1 - bodyfat%). Uses lean mass, so requires an accurate body-fat estimate.
- Cunningham RMR = 500 + 22 * LBM_kg. Runs higher than Katch-McArdle; best for lean endurance athletes (predicted within ~158 kcal/d men, ~103 kcal/d women in one athlete study) but largest mean deviation (~-16.6%) in general populations.
- TDEE = BMR * activity factor (sedentary 1.2, light 1.375, moderate 1.55, active 1.725, very active 1.9) OR, better, BMR + measured activeEnergyBurned from HealthKit (avoids the multiplier guess).
- MET calorie formula (2024 Adult Compendium): kcal/min = MET * 3.5 * body_mass_kg / 200; equivalently kcal = MET * 3.5 * kg/200 * minutes. 1 MET defined as 3.5 ml O2/kg/min resting VO2.
- Corrected MET (Kozey et al.): corrected_MET = standard_MET * (3.5 / measured-or-predicted-RMR-in-ml/kg/min), personalizing for age/sex/body mass via a Harris-Benedict-derived RMR.
- Adaptive TDEE (MacroFactor-style) algorithm: (1) smooth daily bodyweight with an exponentially/rolling weighted moving average to get a weight TREND; (2) over a rolling window (e.g. 14-28 days) compute trend slope in kg/day; (3) energy imbalance kcal/day = trend_slope_kg_per_day * ~7700 kcal/kg (or ~3500 kcal/lb); (4) estimated TDEE = mean_daily_intake - imbalance; (5) recompute continuously so it tracks metabolic adaptation and activity changes.
- Energy density constant: ~7700 kcal per kg of body-weight change (~3500 kcal/lb) is the conversion used to translate weight-trend slope into a calorie surplus/deficit.
- Stanford 2017 (Shcherbina et al., PMC5491979 / MDPI J Pers Med 7(2):3): Apple Watch lowest EE error of 7 devices but ~27% median error; worst device ~92.6%; HR errors 2-6.5%. Devices tested: Apple Watch, Basis Peak, Fitbit Surge, Microsoft Band, Mio Alpha 2, PulseOn, Samsung Gear S2.
- 2024 meta-analysis (~56 studies, models through Series 8): mean calorie overestimation ~28%; hardware improvement only Series 1 ~31.5% error to Series 6 ~26.2%. Series 9/Ultra/Ultra 2 not yet in pooled datasets.
- RMR equation validity: Mifflin-St Jeor within 10% of measured RMR in ~71% of subjects, group-level bias ~0; all equations err in ~1/3 of individuals and worsen at BMI extremes and in athletes (systematic review: no equation reliably accurate for athletes).
- Apple's calorimetry inputs (from Apple's 'Using Apple Watch to measure heart rate, calorimetry, and activity' white paper): PPG heart rate + tri-axial accelerometer + GPS (outdoor) + workout type + user age/sex/height/weight; foreground HR algo uses green LED PPG at 5-second windows during workouts, background uses infrared PPG at 5-minute windows when still; resting HR derived from overnight/still periods.
- Double-count failure example (documented): third-party app wrote a 2200 kcal TDEE into ActiveEnergyBurned; Apple Health added ~500 kcal of Watch active burn on top and displayed 2700 kcal burned. Apple's source-priority for duplicate samples: manual entry > Apple Watch > iPhone motion coprocessor > third-party HealthKit writers.

## Recommendations
- Never write activeEnergyBurned or basalEnergyBurned to HealthKit from this app. READ Apple's samples only. Writing your own estimate is the primary cause of double counting and will corrupt the user's Health data across all apps. For app-computed values, keep them in your own store and label them clearly as your estimate.
- Adopt a MacroFactor-style adaptive TDEE as the app's PRIMARY expenditure engine: back-calculate TDEE from smoothed weight-trend + logged intake over a rolling 14-28 day window (imbalance = trend_slope_kg/day * 7700). This is the most honest number because it self-corrects and doesn't depend on the Watch's ~28%-inflated calorie estimate. Use Mifflin-St Jeor as the cold-start seed for the first ~2 weeks before enough data exists.
- Present energy balance as a TREND, not a daily verdict. Make the hero number a 7-day / rolling-average net balance and weight-trend line; show the single-day in/out only as secondary, explicitly flagged as noisy. Copy: 'Daily numbers are estimates; the multi-week trend is what's real.' This matches both the science and the minimal Anthropic aesthetic (one calm trend line, not a busy daily scoreboard).
- For the 'calories out' display, show Apple's resting + active split transparently but annotate active energy as an estimate that likely runs high (roughly +20-30%). Do NOT let the user add exercise calories back to their intake target (the classic MyFitnessPal double-count trap); if using adaptive TDEE, exercise is already captured by the weight trend, so present a single daily calorie TARGET rather than 'eat back' math.
- Let the user pick a BMR equation but default to Mifflin-St Jeor (best general-population accuracy). Offer Katch-McArdle / Cunningham only when a trustworthy body-fat % exists (e.g. from a smart scale via HealthKit bodyFatPercentage); otherwise LBM-based formulas add error, not precision.
- Use MET-based estimates (2024 Adult Compendium formula) only as a labeled fallback for manually-logged workouts the Watch didn't record, and consider the corrected-MET personalization. Always show these as 'estimated,' never blended silently into trusted Watch data.
- Build one honest 'energy balance' screen: (weight trend line) + (rolling net balance) + (adaptive TDEE estimate with a confidence/settling indicator for the first 2 weeks) + (today's intake vs adaptive target). Avoid precise-looking single-calorie figures; round and show ranges/bands to communicate real uncertainty, which is both more truthful and more on-brand (calm, minimal, non-anxious).
